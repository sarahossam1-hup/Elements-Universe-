01 / THE CASE

WHEN THE SEA BECAME A SOURCE OF DANGER

A beautiful bay.
A hidden danger.

Minamata Disease was first officially reported in May 1956 in Minamata City, Kumamoto Prefecture, Japan.

The disease was caused by methylmercury released in industrial wastewater. The substance entered the aquatic food web, accumulated in fish and shellfish, and eventually affected people who consumed contaminated seafood.

A case that changed the way the world thinks about pollution.

01

THE CONTAMINATION PATH

Industrial Wastewater

Methylmercury was produced as a by-product during chemical manufacturing at the Chisso factory and was released in wastewater.

SCIENCE NOTE

Methylmercury can accumulate in aquatic organisms. Larger fish may contain higher concentrations as the substance moves through the food web.

04 / THE INDUSTRIAL SOURCE

THE FACTORY BEHIND THE CASE

Chisso
Corporation

The Chisso chemical factory in Minamata was identified as the source of methylmercury pollution. Wastewater carried the contaminant into the surrounding aquatic environment.

05 / HISTORICAL TIMELINE

A LONG ROAD TO RECOGNITION

The Events That Changed Everything

1956
THE FIRST REPORT

Minamata Disease is officially identified

The first official report of patients with an unknown neurological illness was made in Minamata City.

1956
SCIENTIFIC INVESTIGATION

Researchers investigate seafood

Kumamoto University researchers reported that the disease was likely linked to heavy-metal poisoning through fish and shellfish.

1965
ANOTHER OUTBREAK

Niigata Minamata Disease

A similar outbreak was reported in the Agano River basin in Niigata Prefecture.

1968
OFFICIAL ANNOUNCEMENT

The cause is officially recognized

The Japanese government announced that methylmercury from industrial wastewater caused Minamata Disease.

1990
ENVIRONMENTAL RESTORATION

Bay restoration project completed

A major project to remove contaminated sediment from parts of Minamata Bay was completed.

06 / HUMAN IMPACT
HISTORICAL EXHIBITION · MINAMATA

BEYOND THE ENVIRONMENT

The Human
Cost

Minamata Disease affects the nervous system. Its effects can include sensory disturbances, difficulty coordinating movement, and problems with vision and hearing.

The disaster affected patients, families, fishing communities, and the wider local society. Its consequences continued long after the pollution was first discovered.

REMEMBERING THE PEOPLE

This case is not only a story about pollution. It is also a story about human lives and the importance of protecting them.
