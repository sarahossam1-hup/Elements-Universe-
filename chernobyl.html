WHY CHERNOBYL HAPPENED

Understanding the engineering failures, operational conditions, and human decisions behind the Chernobyl disaster.

01
THE ROOT OF THE DISASTER

It Was Not One Mistake.

The Chernobyl disaster resulted from a combination of serious reactor design flaws, unstable operating conditions, inadequate safety procedures, and organizational failures.

01 / DESIGN FAILURE

RBMK Reactor Design Flaws

The RBMK reactor had a dangerous positive void coefficient under certain operating conditions. As steam bubbles formed in the coolant, reactor reactivity could increase instead of decrease.

POSITIVE VOID COEFFICIENT
02 / THE EXPERIMENT

The Safety Test

Engineers wanted to determine whether the turbine's remaining rotational energy could temporarily power essential cooling pumps after an electrical supply interruption.

TURBINE RUN-DOWN TEST
03 / UNSTABLE CONDITIONS

Reactor Instability

The reactor entered an unstable low-power operating state. Its control configuration and operating conditions increased the difficulty of maintaining safe control.

REACTIVITY INSTABILITY
04 / SAFETY FAILURES

Operational and Organizational Decisions

Safety procedures were compromised, and the operating team did not have a complete understanding of important reactor design characteristics. Management and safety culture failures also contributed.

SAFETY CULTURE FAILURE
05 / THE CONSEQUENCE

The Reactor Destruction

A rapid and extreme increase in reactor power led to destructive explosions, severe damage to Unit 4, and the release of radioactive materials into the environment.

APRIL 26, 1986
INVESTIGATION CONCLUSION

A Chain of Failures.

Chernobyl was not simply an operator error. It was the outcome of interacting technical, operational, and institutional failures.
