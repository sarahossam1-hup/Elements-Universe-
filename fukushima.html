01 / THE CASE

A NATURAL DISASTER. A NUCLEAR EMERGENCY.

One day.
A chain of events.

On 11 March 2011, a magnitude 9.0 earthquake struck off the northeastern coast of Japan. The earthquake triggered a massive tsunami that reached the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Station.

Flooding damaged emergency power systems. Without enough electricity, essential cooling systems stopped working. This led to severe damage in three operating reactor cores.

A disaster that revealed the importance of preparing for events that can happen together.

EVENT SEQUENCE 01

HOW THE ACCIDENT DEVELOPED

The Earthquake

On 11 March 2011, a magnitude 9.0 earthquake struck off the northeastern coast of Japan. The operating reactors automatically shut down.

SCIENCE NOTE

Nuclear reactors continue producing decay heat after shutdown. Cooling systems are therefore essential even after the chain reaction has stopped.

04 / THE POWER STATION

INSIDE THE ACCIDENT

When Safety
Systems Failed

The tsunami flooded important electrical equipment and emergency generators. The loss of power affected the systems responsible for removing heat from the reactors.

The reactor cores in Units 1, 2 and 3 overheated. Hydrogen accumulated in reactor buildings, leading to explosions in Units 1, 3 and 4.

The disaster begins

A magnitude 9.0 earthquake strikes Japan. The tsunami floods the Fukushima Daiichi site and damages emergency power systems.

UNIT 1

First hydrogen explosion

An explosion damages the upper structure of the Unit 1 reactor building.

14 MAR
UNIT 3

A hydrogen explosion occurs at the Unit 3 reactor building, causing further damage.

An explosion occurs at Unit 4, while damage is also reported around the Unit 2 containment system.

Japanese authorities announce that the damaged reactors have reached a condition of cold shutdown.

06 / ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT

BEYOND THE POWER STATION

The Environmental
Consequences

Radioactive materials were released from the damaged plant into the environment. Some were carried through the atmosphere and deposited on land and the ocean.

Monitoring, food restrictions, environmental assessment, and decontamination became important parts of the long-term response.

ENVIRONMENTAL RESPONSIBILITY

Nuclear safety requires planning for natural hazards, protecting ecosystems, and monitoring environmental conditions.

07 / HUMAN IMPACT
THE GREAT EAST JAPAN EARTHQUAKE · 2011

THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE NUMBERS

Lives
Changed Forever

The earthquake and tsunami caused enormous loss of life and widespread destruction across northeastern Japan. The nuclear accident also led to evacuation orders for communities around the plant.

Displacement, interrupted daily life, stress, and difficulties returning home affected many residents for years.

REMEMBERING THE COMMUNITIES

The consequences of a disaster are measured not only in damaged buildings, but also in the lives and communities affected by it.
